# LinkedIn Commenting

Commenting is described by Mohamed Anis as a **critical weapon** for relationship building and business growth — not a nice-to-have. Regular, high-quality commenting on LinkedIn is the single most reliable way to drive profile views from senior executives at target accounts, build authentic credibility, and create the familiarity that makes pitching possible later.

> *"Commenting is a critical weapon for business growth. Senior leaders who fail to comment on designated posts within 24 hours are displaying poor leadership discipline."*
> — Mohamed Anis, Sep 16 2026

---

## The 24-Hour Commenting Rule

For all leadership team members: when Mohamed Anis or any designated team member publishes a LinkedIn post, every leader must comment within **24 hours**.

This is not a courtesy — it is a discipline standard. Anis flagged the failure of the leadership team to comment within 24 hours on a designated post (Sep 16) as indicative of poor leadership across the board. Common excuses — "the post went up late," "we were waiting for worthwhile content" — were rejected.

**Evidence that commenting works:** In the Sep 17 session, it was confirmed that senior executives at target IT services accounts (Cognizant, Infosys) viewed Ahmed Farhan's profile shortly after his public commenting activity. Commenting triggers profile views from exactly the people you need to reach.

## Human vs AI Comments

This is now a core training topic. The leadership team has moved away from relying on AI tools for LinkedIn comments after a systematic review on Sep 16 revealed that AI-generated comments are immediately detectable.

### Patterns that signal AI generation

Mohamed Anis identified these AI tells during the Sep 16 review:

- Overly complex or sophisticated phrasing where a human would write simply
- Rigid structural patterns (e.g., always using a 3-part framework)
- Corporate buzzwords and jargon phrases like "deeper tension," "catalyze growth"
- Perfect grammar and structure — real humans make small mistakes
- Generic "great post!" framing followed by paraphrasing the original content
- Comments that could apply to any post on the same topic (not specific to what was actually written)

### What authentic human comments look like

- Short, direct, conversational — 8 to 18 words is the target range for executive-level positioning
- Reference something specific in the post that a skimmer wouldn't catch
- Add a genuine observation, reaction, or personal connection
- Deliberate small imperfections and natural connectors ("I've seen this too", "this is exactly right")
- Observational framing and personal realization ("I didn't realize until...")
- Written as if the person is speaking, not writing an essay

Mohamed Anis to Nuuh Iftin (Sep 16): *"This is clearly AI. A human being never uses the phrase 'deeper tension' in a comment. And no real person structures a comment in three exactly equal paragraphs."*

## The Commenting Skill (Sep 18)

Following the Sep 16 review, significant effort went into building a systematic commenting capability. By Sep 18 the team had developed two overlapping tools:

### Farah Ibrahim's Commenting Skill (Foundational)

A custom AI skill built to generate executive-level LinkedIn comments. Stored in a shared document for team access.

**Parameters:**
- **Length:** 8–18 words per comment
- **Elements:** human touch, conversational connectors, deliberate imperfections
- **Framing techniques:** observational framing ("I've noticed..."), personal realization ("I only realized this when..."), specificity (reference actual content in the post)
- **Evaluation method:** generate 3+ comment options, evaluate and select the strongest — the act of evaluating builds critical judgment over time

### Mohamed Anis's Advanced Commenting Skill (18 Techniques)

A more comprehensive system with 18 distinct commenting techniques organized into four families:

| Family | Purpose |
|--------|---------|
| **Build** | Extend or deepen the post's idea with your own take |
| **Bound** | Set a scope or caveat — agree with conditions |
| **Challenge** | Respectfully push back or complicate the point |
| **Connect** | Relate the post to a personal experience, your work, or another domain |

**How it works:** feed the LinkedIn post into the system, which evaluates post relevance and returns the **top 3 recommended techniques** from the 18, with suggested comment angles. Processing takes approximately 3 minutes.

Mohamed Anis's plan (Sep 18): finalize and release this skill to the full team by the morning after the session.

## Content Virality and Second/Third-Degree Reach

From Sep 17 session:

True content virality on LinkedIn does not depend only on your direct (first-degree) network. It depends on **second- and third-degree engagement** — when people who are connected to your connections (but not you) engage with a post, the post reaches their networks in turn.

**Practical implication:** commenting on posts within target accounts — even when the original poster is not yet a first-degree connection — expands visibility into exactly the networks you want to reach. A comment on a Cognizant executive's post will be seen by other Cognizant executives. This is a deliberate targeting mechanism, not just relationship maintenance.

**Optimal posting strategy (from Sep 17):**
- Focus content on specific professional niches rather than general topics
- Optimize for timing — post when your target audience is most active
- Comments on others' posts should be as strategically chosen as your own posts — every comment is a visibility event in that post's network
