# LinkedIn Outreach Messaging

Sending a LinkedIn connection message or direct message requires critical judgment — not just AI execution. The Oct 5 session built an entire framework around evaluating, selecting, and crafting outreach messages at different sophistication levels.

---

## The Foundation: Understand Before Sending

The most common failure mode in LinkedIn outreach is **blind AI execution**: prompting a tool, getting a message, and sending it without critically reading what it actually says.

Mohamed Anis (Oct 5, reviewing Nuuh Iftin's CrowdStrike message):

> *"The most intelligent person I know spent one minute reading this and sent a very stupid message. Your judgment failed you. You are not able to judge whether this is a good one or a bad one."*

Before sending any outreach message, answer two questions:

1. **What is the literal meaning of this message?** Not the objective — the actual meaning of each sentence. Can you explain it in simple terms to a colleague?
2. **Would you send this to this person, in this context, for the first time?** Imagine meeting them at an event. Would you open with this?

If you cannot answer both clearly, do not send. Return to the AI, explain the problem, and ask for a better version.

## Prospect Research: Size Them Up First

Before drafting any message, research the target's LinkedIn profile long enough to understand their context. What you need to know:

- Who are they? (Title, company, what they actually do)
- Why are they at this company? (Founder? Acquired? Hire?)
- What is their trajectory? (Big to small? Small to big? Stuck?)
- What is unusual or interesting about their story?

**The Bill Gates principle:** When you know someone's context deeply (20 years of reading), the question you can ask is instantly powerful. When you only know their last two job titles, you have to work harder to find the interesting angle.

**Rosanna Martinez case study (Oct 5):** Nuuh Iftin sent a message about "scaling outbound across two roles" without understanding that Rosanna had not changed jobs — she was still at both companies simultaneously, because CrowdStrike had *acquired* Onum. The message read as confused because the sender had not sized her up. The correct angle: she was a founding manager at Onum (a big fish in a small pond), and when CrowdStrike acquired Onum, she became a sales development manager (a small fish in a big pond). That transition — from founder-adjacent to absorbed employee — is a powerful, specific, human angle to open with.

## The 3-Tier LinkedIn Messaging System

Mohamed Anis categorized LinkedIn connection and outreach messages into three tiers. Each tier has different acceptance rates, risk profiles, and requirements for the sender.

### Level 1 — Lightweight Acknowledgment

**What it is:** Polite, low-effort interactions. Congratulating on a promotion, acknowledging a birthday or work anniversary, commenting on a recent post, liking and following up with a simple message.

**Acceptance rate:** High — most recipients respond positively because there is no threatening ask.

**Who uses it:** Team members early in their ABSM career, or anyone whose profile does not yet carry enough authority to attempt Level 3.

**Risk:** Low rejection, but also low impact. The recipient will not remember you with particular respect. You are warming a relationship, not establishing yourself as a peer.

**Example angle:** *"Saw your post about [specific thing]. Really resonated. Would love to connect."*

---

### Level 2 — Contextual Relevance

**What it is:** A message that demonstrates you have researched the target and found a specific, relevant angle — a post they published, an event they attended, a shared professional interest. Still warm and non-threatening.

**Acceptance rate:** Moderate — noticeably lower than Level 1, but the response rate from those who do accept is higher.

**Who uses it:** Mid-tier team members who can execute research and frame a clear angle.

**Example angle:** Noting that the target attended a specific conference and asking about a topic discussed there.

---

### Level 3 — Intellectual Contrast / Peer Positioning

**What it is:** A message that immediately establishes intellectual peer equality or superiority. It references something specific about the target's career trajectory — often a tension, a paradox, or a surprising transition — and asks a question that demonstrates deep understanding.

**Acceptance rate:** Lower than Level 1. But when it works, it creates an immediate perception of the sender as formidable.

**Who can use it:** Anyone, if they have done the research. The message does not require seniority — it requires insight.

**Risk:** Farah Ibrahim raised the concern in the Oct 5 session that highly personalized Level 3 messages might appear calculated or "stalker-like." Mohamed Anis's response: recipients who receive genuine intellectual engagement do not experience it as stalking — they experience it as finally meeting someone worth talking to.

**Example from Oct 5:** Using Rosanna Martinez's acquisition story — going from founding manager at Onum (own company, own culture, own mandate) to SDM at CrowdStrike (absorbed, reporting up the hierarchy) — as the hook. The question is not "what changed about your outbound strategy?" (generic) but something more like: *"Going from founding a BD motion from scratch to having one handed to you — does that feel like acceleration or constraint?"*

This is the kind of question that makes someone stop and think: *"This person actually gets it."*

## AI Output Evaluation: The Missing Step

AI tools generate plausible-sounding messages that may be:

- Confused about the prospect's actual situation
- Asking inappropriate questions for a first contact
- Peer-to-peer networking questions dressed up as sales openers

**The evaluation process:**

1. Read the AI output out loud. Does it make sense?
2. Identify the literal question being asked. Can you explain it simply?
3. Ask: would this be weird to say to someone you just met at an event?
4. If anything is off — go back to the AI: *"This message is asking [X]. Is that appropriate for a first contact? What's a better angle?"*

Mohamed Anis's teaching: the value of AI tools is not their first output. The value is the back-and-forth — using the AI to evaluate its own output, challenge it, and iterate until the message is genuinely strong.

## Connection vs Message Strategy

Two distinct moves on LinkedIn:

**Blank connection request** — send with no note. High acceptance rate. Low perception impact. The relationship building happens *after* the connection.

**Targeted connection message** — a Level 2 or Level 3 message sent with the connection request. Lower acceptance rate. Higher perception impact when accepted.

For most outbound hunters in early ABSM: **blank first, message after acceptance.** The blank connection gets you in the door; the subsequent message is where you make your move.
