# Talent Formula

The Talent Formula emerged from the Sep 17 Daily Learning session as a framework for understanding professional development, evaluating potential hires in Kakuma, and explaining to underserved learners why starting now — even without credentials — is the right move.

---

## The Formula

```
Talent × Effort = Skill
Skill × Effort = Achievement
```

**Talent** is raw, natural aptitude — the baseline capacity you bring to any domain. It is fixed (you either have it or you don't in a given area) but latent: talent without effort produces nothing.

**Effort** is the multiplier. The same talent applied with more effort produces dramatically more skill. Two people with identical talent but different effort levels will diverge over time — one compounds, the other stagnates.

**Skill** is the tangible, demonstrable result of talent × effort. Skill is what an employer pays for. It is observable, verifiable, and marketable.

**Achievement** is skill compounded by continued effort — career growth, income growth, professional recognition.

### Why this matters for the underserved

The talent formula reframes the conversation about "qualifications":

- Traditional hiring asks for proof of prior achievement (degrees, brand-name employers)
- The talent formula says: *if the talent is real and the effort is applied, the skill will come*
- The bottleneck is not talent — it is access to environments that reward sustained effort

For learners in Kakuma or similar contexts, the insight is that the absence of credentials does not mean the absence of talent. It means the absence of opportunity to demonstrate talent through effort.

## The Proof Trap

A structural challenge for all underserved job-seekers: **to get experience, you need experience.**

Employers in global technology companies require proof of prior work — often at a brand-name organization. Without that proof, even highly talented candidates are filtered out before a human ever reads their application.

The proof trap is not solvable by talent alone:
- **No brand-name employer on the CV** → filtered by automated systems before a human sees the application
- **No proof of capability** → interviewers have no anchor for evaluating unconventional candidates
- **No network** → referrals are how most jobs in knowledge work are actually filled

**The StepUp.One response:** provide an environment where underserved candidates can build demonstrable skill (through Daily Learning sessions, ABSM campaigns, CRM work) and a track record of real outcomes — transforming the situation from "no proof" to "proof from a non-traditional context."

## The Infosys Benchmark

Infosys (100,000+ employees, one of the world's largest IT services companies) was used in the Sep 17 session to illustrate what professional-grade talent development looks like at scale.

| Dimension | Infosys Standard |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Annual graduate recruitment | 10,000 graduates per year |
| Hiring filters | Strict academic cutoffs, common sense exams, rigorous interviews |
| Training investment | $200 million on a 6-month corporate university program |
| Hiring cost | $100 million annually on recruitment alone |
| Total annual talent investment | $300 million |

**What this tells us:** the world's most competitive professional environments are built on a foundation of structured, intensive, multi-month training — not just innate ability. Infosys does not assume graduates arrive job-ready. They rebuild them from the ground up.

**The StepUp.One parallel:** the Daily Learning sessions are StepUp.One's version of the corporate university. The sessions are intense, repetitive (the same concepts taught until internalized), and designed to build professional habit alongside knowledge.

## Earnings Trajectory: The Argument for Skills Investment

From the Sep 17 session, a concrete comparison that illustrates why skills investment beats immediate income maximization:

| Path | Year 1 Income | Year 10 Income |
|------|--------------|----------------|
| Delivery driver | 40,000–80,000 KSh/month | ~40,000–80,000 KSh/month (stagnant) |
| Entry-level software engineer | 20,000 KSh/month | 2,000,000 KSh/month (compounding) |

The delivery driver out-earns the engineer in year one. By year ten, the engineer earns 25x more. The formula plays out: the engineer's talent × effort over 10 years compounded skill, which compounded achievement.

This comparison is intended as a persuasion tool for underserved learners who are tempted to abandon skills development for immediate income.

## Identifying Talent in Kakuma

Traditional talent identification tools (academic scores, IQ tests, credential screening) do not work well in Kakuma or similar underserved contexts. The Sep 17 session identified an alternative framework:

**What to measure:**
1. **Learnability** — how quickly does the candidate absorb new concepts? Do they ask clarifying questions? Do they apply what they've learned to new situations?
2. **Effort** — consistency, follow-through, willingness to repeat until mastery. The formula requires effort; candidates who apply effort irregularly will not compound.

**What not to rely on:**
- Academic scores (reflect access to educational resources, not raw capability)
- IQ tests (culturally biased, poorly predictive of performance in novel professional contexts)
- Prior job titles (proof trap — absence of experience ≠ absence of talent)

**Practical identification method:** design an evaluation process that measures learnability and effort directly. Put candidates in a learning situation, observe how fast they absorb it and how persistently they engage with it. This is more predictive than any credential.

## Nature + Nurture: What Talent Actually Is

From the Sep 21 session, Anis clarified that talent is not purely innate — it has two components:

- **Nature** — fixed genetics; biological limits on what is achievable in a given domain. Example: no non-Black runner has won the Olympic 100m since 1980 — genetic makeup creates real performance limits that effort alone cannot overcome.
- **Nurture** — environment, upbringing, exposure, and accumulated practice. This is the part that can be shaped.

Effort is entirely within a person's control. Talent is not — it is a combination of what you were born with and what environment you were exposed to.

The practical implication: talent is latent and may never reveal itself without the right environment. The absence of discovered talent does not mean the absence of talent.

## Talent Discovery: The 5-Step Framework

Talent cannot be assessed before it has been given a chance to emerge. The sequence from the Sep 21 session (using the boxing analogy where Mahmud Bilow coaches 500 candidates in Kakuma for the Olympics):

1. **Hunger** — the candidate must have desire for the opportunity, even before knowing whether they have talent. Marwa, a new mother who had never boxed, showed up because the potential upside was transformative (Olympic champion → millionaire). Hunger precedes everything.
2. **Exposure** — before asking for commitment, show what is possible. Play videos of champions. Tell inspiring stories. Let people understand what boxing is before asking them to commit to it. Exposure creates informed desire.
3. **Capability commitment** — after exposure, announce the non-negotiable requirements: 6 hours/day, 7 days/week, 4 years, strict attendance. Participants self-select. Those who stay have demonstrated willingness to commit.
4. **Training begins** — only now does actual training start. At this point, neither the coach nor the candidate knows whether the candidate has talent.
5. **Talent emerges** — after approximately **3 months** of intensive daily training, natural talent becomes visible. Marwa discovered her boxing talent after 3 months at 6 hours/day (reaching ~5% skill level). Her coach could see it. She could feel it. Before 3 months, the assessment is premature.

**Key insight:** you cannot discover your talent without first passing through all five steps in order. Credential-based filtering (requiring prior experience or qualifications before training begins) eliminates candidates who might have the talent but have never been given the conditions to find it.

## The Talent Timeline

From the Sep 21 session, using Marwa's boxing progression as the template:

| Phase | Duration | What Happens |
|-------|----------|-------------|
| Discovery (5 steps above) | ~3 months | Talent identified; ~5% skill level |
| Skill development | 3–24 months | Internal sparring, no external fights; skills compound |
| 100% skill | Month 24 | Coach declares Marwa ready for external competition |
| Achievement ladder | Year 3+ | Kenya champion → Africa champion → Olympic champion → World champion |

The second formula (`Skill × Effort = Achievement`) only begins when training is complete. The first years are pure investment. Achievement is downstream of skill; skill is downstream of effort; effort must be applied long enough to reveal and compound talent.

## Deliberate Practice vs Initial Training

Initial training and deliberate practice are two distinct phases:

**Initial training** (months 1–3):
- Purpose: establish fundamentals and discover whether talent exists
- Measurement: talent identification, not outcome
- What to expect: slow progress, low skill levels, frequent failure
- The 3-month period is when the talent question gets answered

**Deliberate practice** (month 3 onward):
- Requires **measurable goals** — not "I practiced today" but "I averaged 1,000 impressions across 10 comments"
- Incorporates systematic video recording of performance
- Includes self-reflection after each session
- Requires coach feedback on specific observations
- Practice without measurement is activity, not deliberate practice — it does not compound skill

The failure to transition from training to deliberate practice is the single most common reason skilled people plateau. Practice without measurable outcomes is not effort in the equation's sense.

## AI-Graded Talent Test (Commenting Application)

From the Sep 21 session, a concrete method for evaluating talent in domains where physical measurement is unavailable (unlike boxing, where punch speed and strength are directly measurable):

**The method:**
1. Take a LinkedIn post
2. Generate 5 pre-written comment options, graded 1 (worst) to 5 (best) by AI
3. Ask the candidate to select the comment they believe is best — using only their initial judgment, before any explanation
4. Score based on the **first choice**, not the corrected choice
5. Repeat across 100+ examples to establish a talent percentage

**Why the first choice matters:** if a candidate always selects the best option after seeing the AI's reasoning, that measures nothing — it measures their ability to follow instructions, not their raw judgment. The first, unaided choice reveals the natural talent level.

**Probability baseline:** a random "monkey" picking without reading would score approximately 25% (randomly selecting from 4 options). A very lucky monkey might reach 50%. Scoring below 25% consistently is nearly impossible; scoring 100% consistently is the mark of exceptional commenting talent.

**Talent scores:**
| Score | Talent Level |
|-------|-------------|
| ~25% | Random (no talent signal) |
| ~50% | Average |
| 75–100% | Strong talent |
| Consistently 0% | Nearly impossible |
| Consistently 100% | Possible; signals high natural aptitude |

This method is applicable to any domain where "picking the best option" is the core skill — not just commenting. Anis noted this represents the future of learning: AI generates the options; human talent is the ability to select correctly among them.
