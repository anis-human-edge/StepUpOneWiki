# ABSM Landing Page

The ABSM landing page at [stepup.one/ABSM](https://stepup.one/ABSM) is StepUp.One's primary sales asset for the Account-Based Sales and Marketing service. It is the destination for all paid traffic, cold outreach, and LinkedIn campaigns — Anis's framing: "10,000 people are going to land on this page in the next one year and our entire future depends on how good this page looks."

The page was published on 2026-08-09 (Saturday, immediately after the Aug 9 session) and was publicly reviewed by the team in the Aug 10 Daily Learning session. As of Aug 10, it was on its 10th revision.

## Page Structure

The page is organized in sections, each with a specific emotional/informational job:

| Section | Purpose |
|---------|---------|
| Section 1: The Problem | Establish why ABSM matters for all three personas (startup / scaleup / enterprise). Emotion: "you live and die by your accounts." |
| Section 2: The Solution | Account-based relationship building — bottom-up, one account at a time. Positioned as the single answer to all three pain points. |
| Section 3: ABSM Scenarios | Three specific ABSM applications: Getting Invited (startup), Cracking the Deal (scaleup), Protect & Grow (enterprise) |
| CTA: Run the Test | "Give us one account. In two weeks you'll see the full picture." → booking link |
| About section | Anis's positioning and StepUp.One story |

## Aug 10 Feedback: What the Team Said

**Farah Ibrahim:**
- Page is too bulky — too much to read; target audience won't read long-form before understanding the value
- CTA mismatch: "Run the test on one account" leads to a booking link, but the visitor expects a product demo page or a feature overview, not a founder's calendar
- Startups and scaleups should not appear in the same sentence — the persona is too broad; pick one and be precise
- Distinction: there is a difference between *simplicity* and *clarity*. The goal is clarity, not dumbing it down.

**Ahmed Farhan:**
- Prefers the previous fundraising landing page structure: three playbooks (relationship building, warm hunting, signal hunting), very clean, 1–3 scrolls
- ABSM is not a globally known term — ABM is; always write out "Account-Based Sales and Marketing" before abbreviating
- Agreed the page is too long for busy B2B executives

**Abdulkadir Mhina:**
- Too many promises — flagged as risky (though Anis clarified that the "two-week" promise is an account mapping exercise: use Sales Navigator to show all stakeholders in one target account and who the client is already connected to — a 15-minute task, not a complicated deliverable)
- Booking link appears twice (once in the hero, once at the bottom) — flagged as repetitive

**Anis's response and direction:**
- Defended all three personas: ABSM is critical for a single-person company trying to sell an LLM to a 100,000-person enterprise *and* for an enterprise protecting $100M accounts. The persona is "any B2B company whose revenue depends on winning, keeping, or growing large accounts."
- Confirmed the two-week promise is the account mapping call — show the client exactly which connections they have and don't have in one target account; this is done in Sales Navigator in minutes.
- Direction: team to give feedback *section by section*, not holistically. For each section: does it work? Does it need to be revised, revamped, or removed?
- Section 1 goal reframed: whether you're a startup (not invited to the table), scaleup (losing at the committee stage), or enterprise (protecting $100M accounts) — your pain is real, your problem is the same, and ABSM is the answer.

## Proposed Revisions (Aug 10)

1. **Reduce length** — cut or collapse sections; aim for 1–3 scrolls max on section 1
2. **Define ABSM on first use** — write "Account-Based Sales and Marketing (ABSM)" before using the abbreviation
3. **Separate or clarify the three personas** — don't mix them in one sentence; show each pain point distinctly and let the reader self-select
4. **Combine sections 2 and 3** — the problem (which account are you thinking about?) and the ABSM scenario (breaking in vs. cracking the deal) should map 1:1; the middle "which account are you thinking about" section was flagged as confusing and a candidate for removal
5. **Fix CTA mismatch** — "run the test on one account" CTA should either go to a page explaining the account mapping process or be re-labeled as a "book a strategy call" CTA
6. **Profile picture** — Farah noted the sample profile picture used on the page is worse than Anis's real LinkedIn profile photo; needs upgrading

## The "Give Us One Account" Promise

This is the key offering on the page — the proof-of-concept CTA:

> "Give us one account. In two weeks you will see the full picture."

**What it delivers:**
1. Client names a target account (e.g., Siemens)
2. StepUp.One pulls all stakeholders using LinkedIn Sales Navigator: C-suite, VPs, directors, by department, country, and city
3. Cross-references with client's existing connections — shows who is already connected (say 20 out of 200) and who remains unconnected (180)
4. Delivers a prioritized connection gap map — these are the people you need to connect with first

This is a 15-minute exercise, not a weeks-long engagement. The two-week promise is conservative. The deliverable is self-evidently valuable: the client immediately sees the scale of the relationship gap in their most important account.
