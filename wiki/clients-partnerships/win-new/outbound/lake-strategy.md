# Lake Strategy & CNT Framework

Accelerator-based prospecting framework where "lakes" are accelerators/incubators and "fish" are founders — extended with the CNT principle that governs when and how to engage.

## The CNT Principle

**Context, Need, and Timing** — all three must be aligned to win a client. This is the core formula behind StepUp.One's outbound success.

> "If you know the context, if there is a clear need, and if you time it perfectly, you can catch fish every day. This is the formula." — Mohamed Anis, June 2026

- **Context** — who is this person and why are they in this space? Anyone who goes near an accelerator is there for one reason: funding. That's their context.
- **Need** — what are they desperately trying to solve? Founders in accelerators need capital. That need is explicit and urgent.
- **Timing** — when to engage. The moment they post or comment in the accelerator's community space is the moment to reach out — they are in the market right now.

Without all three, outreach backfires. With all three, it feels helpful rather than intrusive. The same person at the wrong moment will think you're a thief; at the right moment they will thank you.

### The Vegetable Market Metaphor

If someone is simply walking down a road, asking whether they want to buy vegetables is intrusive. But inside the vegetable market, while they are picking carrots, saying "the other shop has these cheaper" feels like a gift. The context (they're in the market), the need (they want vegetables), and the timing (they are mid-purchase) make the same message welcome.

Applied to accelerators: if a founder comments on a Techstars post, that is the moment to engage. They are mid-activity in the accelerator space, so any message about raising capital or finding clients feels natural, not cold.

### Universality

The CNT principle applies beyond founders: fractional executives, board members, investors, and any other ICP each have their own "vegetable market." The team's job is to identify the CNT for each new target segment.

## The Lake and Fish Model

- **Lake** = an accelerator, incubator, or investor firm (YC, Techstars, Plug and Play, etc.)
- **Fish** = prospects associated with that lake
- **Hungry fish** = the critical selector — not just any lake with many fish, but a lake where the fish are actively surfacing, seeking something

A lake with a million well-fed fish (e.g., Y Combinator's massive alumni network) is less valuable than a smaller lake with founders actively raising capital right now. Y Combinator alumni are focused; their tummies are full. They will not rise to the bait. The right lake is one where the fish are hungry and at the surface.

The two dimensions to evaluate a lake:
1. **Presence** — how many fish are there?
2. **Hunger** — are they actively seeking what we offer right now?

## Two Types of Fish

1. **Founders already funded (inside the lake)** — standard fundraising outreach does not work; they are beyond the capital-seeking stage. Angle: execution — "You have been given a lot of strategy; how many are you executing every day?"
2. **Founders actively seeking entry (outside the lake)** — prime targets; CNT is perfectly aligned. They are commenting, posting, attending — timing is ideal.

## Accelerator Social Media Engagement Strategy

The highest-leverage execution tactic (June 2026 emphasis):

1. Follow the top 20–100 accelerator and incubator accounts on LinkedIn
2. Monitor their posts daily for comments from founders
3. Reply to founder comments directly — acknowledge what they're doing, position StepUp.One as a complement
4. Then send a connection request and private message

This works because the founder has already demonstrated context, need, and timing by commenting on an accelerator post. The outreach is therefore not cold — it is a continuation of a public conversation they started.

There is no shortage of this pipeline: thousands of founders comment on accelerator content every day worldwide. The lake is effectively infinite.

## Cross-Pollination Prevention

Each team member is assigned to specific lakes and owns them exclusively. Two team members working the same lake simultaneously create "cross-pollination" — the same prospect receives duplicate outreach, damaging credibility and trust. One person, one lake.

## Lake Quality Ranking

1. **Techstars, Mass Challenge, Seed Camp, Antler, Peak** — active comment communities, founders are actively fundraising. High hunger.
2. **Plug and Play** — smaller lake but "advertises their fish," easy to identify and connect within minutes.
3. **Y Combinator** — largest (13,000+ founders), but alumni are well-fed and less responsive to cold outreach. Better for inside-the-lake angles.
4. **Movement 51** — non-profit focused on female entrepreneurs; lower average founder quality, but the incubator itself may be a partnership target.

## Strategic Insight

For lower-quality lakes (e.g., Movement 51), the better play is to connect with the incubator's operators and offer to serve their top 10% of founders directly, rather than hunting individual founders.

## Metrics

Key question per lake: how many fish are inside, how many are actively trying to enter, and are they commenting/posting this week? This determines which messaging angle to use and whether the lake is currently active.
