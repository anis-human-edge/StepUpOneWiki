# ABSM Methodology

**Account Based Sales and Marketing (ABSM)** is StepUp.One's strategic evolution beyond fundraising. It is a long-term, relationship-first GTM methodology structured around three independent engines — Connection, Engagement, and Pitching — each run, measured, and reported separately.

As of August 2026, StepUp.One is pivoting to ABSM as its primary service offering and applying the methodology internally before rolling it out for clients.

## Why ABSM

Fundraising is a temporary service. No client has stayed more than 3 months regardless of results. The effort must be repeated with every new client. ABSM by contrast produces sticky, long-term client relationships because:

- B2B GTM is built on long-term relationships, not transactions
- A company that signs for ABSM stays as long as the methodology is running
- Even mediocre ABSM execution retains clients far longer than excellent fundraising execution
- The wins compound: client relationships build on each other; fundraising is always zero-sum

**The branding advantage:** ABM (Account Based Marketing) is a well-known term. ABSM — Account Based Sales and Marketing — does not exist as a defined concept anywhere else. StepUp.One is defining the word and owning it. This is the same positioning move as creating a brand around "fractional CXO engine" — carve out a word nobody else has.

## Target Company Profile

ABSM is designed for well-funded, revenue-generating companies, not underfunded startups:

| Criterion | Target |
|-----------|--------|
| Revenue | $10M–$100M |
| Capitalization | Well-funded (not early-stage, not pre-revenue) |
| Team | Dedicated sales function (CRO, head of sales, CMO) |
| Industry approach | One industry at a time |

**Avoid:** billion-dollar enterprises (e.g., Accenture, Infosys) — the sales team is too large, the CMO is unreachable, and the account is impossible to cover. Also avoid underfunded startups — they cannot sustain engagement and cannot pay for long-term service.

**Priority industries (start here):** IT services, B2B SaaS.

**Target accounts per campaign:** 100 companies per industry, ~50 stakeholders per company = 5,000 stakeholders per industry.

## The Three Engines

Each engine is independent. Do not move to the next engine until the current one has hit its minimum performance threshold. Report each engine separately in every weekly and monthly review.

### 1. Connection Engine

**Goal:** Connect with at least 80% of identified stakeholders.

If you identify 5,000 stakeholders, you must connect with at least 4,000 before running the Engagement Engine at full scale. A 10–20% connection rate is not a success condition — it means the Connection Engine is still running.

**How to run it:**
- Identify all stakeholders across 100 target accounts.
- Include the client's *existing* LinkedIn connections — not only new outreach targets. If the client has 10,000 existing connections, those count as potentially already in the Connection Engine. Map them.
- Run three separate connection strategies (different message angles, different timing, different senders).
- If three LinkedIn attempts fail, try email.
- Do not advance to Engagement until 80% connected.

**What to report:** connection rate this month vs last month. The graph should always be rising.

### 2. Engagement Engine

**Goal:** Add genuine value to connected stakeholders before asking for anything in return.

**Rules:**
- Wait at least **1 month** after connecting before sending a private message.
- Public engagement (commenting, tagging) is allowed immediately after connection.
- Never blast value-add content as a mass message. Segment by role: one white paper for CROs, a different one for founders, another for CMOs.
- Two rounds of value-add minimum before any pitch conversation.

**What counts as value:** white papers, relevant posts, resources tailored to the prospect's role and company stage. The value must be genuinely useful to the recipient, not a thinly-veiled pitch.

**Why timing matters:** a cold prospect who receives value before you ask for anything feels engaged, not sold to. A cold prospect who receives a pitch on day one feels like a list entry.

**What to report:** engagement rate (replies, thanks, reactions) as a separate metric from connection rate.

### 3. Pitching Engine

**Goal:** Convert engaged relationships into sales conversations — but only when the prospect is ready.

**The hard rules:**
- Only activate after **at least 1 month each** of connection and engagement (minimum 2 months total before pitching; 3 months is the full cycle).
- Pitching cannot occur in the first month — there is zero visibility or historical context with the prospect at that point.
- Pitching too early, before proper nurturing has occurred, effectively invalidates any prior engagement effort.
- Only pitch if the prospect **explicitly asks** what you do.
- If a client instructs "never pitch unless they ask" — hold to that; it works.
- When you do pitch: keep it conversational and pointed, not a formal presentation.

**Why this ordering matters:** a prospect who has been connected and engaged for 1–2 months already knows your name, has seen your value, and likely has an impression of what you do. When they ask, they're already warm. Pitching before this point destroys the trust built by the first two engines.

**What to report:** pitch rate, meeting booking rate, conversion rate — all separate from engagement metrics.

## The ABSM Cycle: 1 Month Per Stage

As refined in the Aug 5 session, the cycle has been tightened from a vague "3-month" window to **1 month per stage**:

| Month | Stage | Activity |
|-------|-------|----------|
| Month 1 | Connection | Connect only — no private messaging, no pitching |
| Month 2 | Engagement | Nurture with value — commenting, white papers, relevant content |
| Month 3 | Pitching | Pitch only when prospect asks; never cold-pitch |

**Why 1 month per stage:** a 30-day gap between stages is sufficient to avoid appearing scripted or robotic to prospects. Longer gaps slow revenue; shorter gaps feel rushed and damage relationship authenticity.

**Existing connections run in parallel:** for a client's *existing* LinkedIn connections (built before the ABSM campaign started), the Engagement Engine can be activated simultaneously from day one — those relationships have already passed the connection threshold.

## Reporting Structure

All three engines must be reported separately. The old model of "how many meetings did we book?" collapses the funnel into a single number that hides where the breakdown is. ABSM reporting shows exactly which engine needs work:

| Engine | Key Metric | Direction |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Connection | % of target stakeholders connected | Must trend toward 80% |
| Engagement | % of connected stakeholders who engaged | Must trend up month over month |
| Pitching | Meetings / pitches / conversion | Measured only after 3 months |

If meetings are low, check: is it the Connection Engine (too few people connected) or the Engagement Engine (connected but cold) or the Pitching Engine (bad pitch)?

## Account Graph (Aspirational)

The full ABSM vision includes an **Account Graph**: a live, real-time view of every relationship between every StepUp.One team member and every stakeholder in every target account, updated weekly.

The account graph answers four questions every Friday:
1. Who are our named accounts?
2. Who are every stakeholder in each account?
3. Who on our team holds each relationship?
4. Which relationships are strong vs. just a name in a database?

The current CRM supports roughly 40–50% of this. The account graph as described in the ABSM working paper requires purpose-built software. StepUp.One's near-term goal is to show a basic version of this — not the full real-time graph — using the CRM tools available.

## Internal Validation Before Client Rollout

Before applying ABSM to client accounts, StepUp.One must run it internally:

1. Define 100 target companies StepUp.One wants to win as clients.
2. Identify stakeholders across those 100 accounts.
3. Map existing connections across all team members to those companies.
4. Run the Connection, Engagement, and Pitching engines exactly as you would for a client — same discipline, same reporting.

The internal run validates the methodology and produces a replicable playbook. You cannot teach a client ABSM if you have never executed it yourself.

**Team structure:** teams are organized by industry. The team running ABSM for IT services is separate from the team running ABSM for B2B SaaS.

## LinkedIn Profile Transformation

All team members and client-facing profiles must be updated to reflect ABSM specialist positioning:

- Remove fundraising-focused language.
- Add ABSM specialist identity (use AI to rewrite: paste the ABSM service page + white paper into Claude or ChatGPT and ask it to rewrite your profile).
- Resources: ABSM service page and ABSM working paper (both published on the StepUp.One website).

## Handling Client-Directed Transactional Requests

ABSM is the standard. But if a client explicitly brings their own strategy and asks for execution only, execute their strategy — do not refuse. If the client dictates the approach, the outcome responsibility shifts to them. Keep the conversation going with ambiguous clients rather than closing the door prematurely.
