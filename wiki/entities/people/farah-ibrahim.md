## Jun 25, 2026: Coordination Conflict and Disempowerment

Farah expressed significant frustration about a perceived coordination failure: he felt that other team members (specifically Abdulkadir Mhina) had acted on his clients' accounts without informing him. He described it as feeling "underestimated as a team leader."

**Abdulkadir Mhina's account:** He had responded to a WhatsApp message from Farah with a laughing emoji, intending it as a joke (they normally joke). Farah interpreted it as dismissiveness. Abdulkadir had been present in the strategy session where the commenting strategy was discussed; Farah had missed it.

**Mohamed Anis's position:** The "unauthorized" actions were part of a planned social media commenting strategy discussed in sessions Farah did not attend. The strategy: comment first on own/StepUp.One posts, then on clients' posts, then on trending posts. Anis told Farah: "You have to trust that I will not do something to jeopardize your client... This client is with you because of me."

**Farah's underlying concern:** He expressed feeling like "a stupid team member who just executes what he's been told" rather than a real team leader. He said he does not feel empowered to say no to decisions about his own clients.

**Anis's advice:** Don't write anything when angry. Come back the next day and discuss calmly. Farah's frustration was acknowledged as valid in terms of communication — the lesson for the team is to inform client leaders before acting on their accounts.

**Resolution:** Farah and Abdulkadir both affirmed there is no personal issue. The coordination protocol is now clearer: inform the responsible team leader before acting on their client accounts, even for strategic reasons.

## Watch-Along Sessions (Jun 2026)

Farah Ibrahim introduced the practice of **watch-along sessions**: the team watches recording videos of Anis's client meetings together, pausing to analyze tactics, ask questions, and discuss in real time.

Example: The team spent two hours on a 20-minute segment of the Lucas video, analyzing how Anis switches from sales mode to relationship building, how he finds common ground, and how he handles objections. Farah described it: "for you it's boring because it's you. But I think for everybody in StepUp.One it's invaluable."

Anis's endorsement: "Whatever I say in 3 hours here, you will gain more in those 30 minutes [of the video]. All my meetings are only 30 minutes. You will figure this out by watching me act in front of the client."

Recommended: all team members to watch the Lewis meeting video in Beacon as "a PhD thesis on pitching."

## Leadership Philosophy (Jun 25)

Farah articulated a position in the leadership debate: team leaders need both commitment AND skill; team members primarily need skill. Leaders should be thrown into difficult situations to develop — Farah cited her own experience: "if you couldn't have thrown me in the river, I would not be ready." She advocated giving leadership roles to committed people even if not fully ready (e.g., Sherogi).
