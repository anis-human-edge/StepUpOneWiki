# Ryan Sullivan

Former StepUp.One team member and academic researcher. Worked with Mohamed Anis for approximately 10 years. Completed a PhD whose thesis was directly informed by his professional relationship with Anis: **turning relationships into outcomes**.

## Background

Ryan worked within StepUp.One's orbit in a role that Anis describes as analogous to the team's current structure — sustained, long-term professional collaboration. His relationship with Bahana (a long-standing team member) is cited as an example of a **transactional work relationship**: professional, dependable, but not yet trusted.

Anis notes that even Ryan and Bahana — colleagues of seven years — sit at the transactional work level. The relationship is valuable but transferable; it could be reassigned without significant damage to either party.

## Academic Work

Ryan's PhD, completed after approximately six years of study, is titled (or themed around) **"Turning Relationships into Outcomes"**. He published a book on this topic.

The core thesis: most professionals have large networks but do not know how to convert those connections into tangible results — revenue, procurement, referrals, introductions. Relationships die through inertia when they are not deliberately converted. This is why 90% of professional relationships remain transactional: people move to trust, but stop before asking for the outcome.

Anis's influence on Ryan's thinking is significant. Much of the framework Ryan formalized in his PhD was developed through their years of working together.

## Relationship to StepUp.One

Ryan is referenced in Daily Learning sessions as a proof point for the relationship → outcomes framework. His book is assigned reading (implicitly — Anis references it with "go to Ryan Sullivan" and has linked it in session notes).

Anis views Ryan's PhD as validation of a framework he had already discovered empirically: that trust is necessary but not sufficient. The final step — converting trust into concrete, large-scale asks — is what most professionals never learn.
