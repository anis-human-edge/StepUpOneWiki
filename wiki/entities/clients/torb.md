# Torb

UK-based healthcare technology client. Makes a visual clinical decision support tool — called the **Torb chart** — that helps doctors and patients jointly make treatment decisions for orthopedic and musculoskeletal (MSK) conditions such as hip and knee problems.

## Product

The Torb chart is a visual roadmap for a specific condition (e.g., hip arthritis). For that condition, it presents all treatment options in a single chart: surgery, injections, physiotherapy, watchful waiting. The doctor and patient sit together and use the chart to decide on the right treatment path.

Benefits:
- Reduces unnecessary surgeries by making non-surgical options explicit
- Reduces hospital resource consumption
- Enables shared decision-making between clinician and patient
- Can be customized for a specific hospital's protocols

Currently scoped to orthopedic and MSK conditions only. There is no Torb chart for cancer, mental health, or other specialties. This means only hospitals with an **orthopedic department** are valid targets.

## Market

**Geography:** UK only — NHS hospitals and private healthcare providers  
**Target institutions:** hospitals with an orthopedic or MSK department  
**End users:** orthopedic surgeons and MSK specialists  
**Buyers:** hospital procurement officers, CFOs, CEOs, surgery directors — NOT individual doctors

## Critical Institutional Compliance Insight

Doctors cannot bring any external tool into a hospital without prior institutional approval — even if it is just a paper chart. This is the same principle that prevents employees from using personal ChatGPT at a company like Infosys: data privacy and security policy require all tools to be institutionally vetted and purchased.

**Implication for ABSM:** target the hospital's buying hierarchy (procurement directors, CFO, CEO, surgery director), not individual orthopedic surgeons. Doctors are **influencers**, not buyers.

## LinkedIn Limitation

Most UK orthopedic consultants do not have LinkedIn profiles, or maintain profiles that are outdated and hard to find. LinkedIn-first outreach is largely ineffective for this campaign. Alternative methods — hospital websites, GMC registrations, specialist society directories, email — are required to reach doctors as influencers.
