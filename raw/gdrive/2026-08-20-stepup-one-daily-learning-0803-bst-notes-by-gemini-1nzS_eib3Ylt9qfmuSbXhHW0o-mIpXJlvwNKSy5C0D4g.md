Mohamed Anis explained that the system does not take months to "deliver results" but rather acts as a continuous, daily process to build familiarity between startups and key decision-makers at large companies. They noted that this relationship-building can happen within weeks or months, and the goal is simply to help the startup get invited to the table.
- **Nurturing Strategy and Risks**: AHMED FARHAN questioned the "no nurturing" phase for the first month of the system, noting the risks involved in nurturing a client's existing network without knowing the history of their prior relationships. Mohamed Anis agreed that there are no "magic" shortcuts and that the team must stick to their established principles of building relationships slowly and steadily, rather than attempting to rush the process for desperate clients.
- **Blog Content Critique and Editing**: The team reviewed the blog content, with Mohamed Anis emphasizing that the writing must avoid long, irrelevant introductions and excessive problem descriptions — such as the "stomach pain" analogy — as these do not add value to intelligent readers who simply want solutions. Mohamed Anis instructed the team to remove filler content, such as the "three types of founders" section, which they deemed nonsensical and characteristic of poor AI-generated text that should be deleted to keep the content concise and impactful.
- **Publishing Status and Review**: Mohamed Anis reported that they had already finalized the blog, created accompanying images, and published the post on their original LinkedIn profile. AHMED FARHAN was tasked with reviewing the published post on LinkedIn to identify and suggest the removal of any remaining useless or "AI-sounding" lines to ensure the content remains high-quality for the intended audience of 50,000 people.
- **Defining Professional Responsibility and Success Rates**: In response to concerns about low success rates, Mohamed Anis clarified that the team takes 100% responsibility for the processes they control, such as building relationships, engaging with the right people, and setting meetings. Mohamed Anis used an analogy of a bridge builder to explain that while they provide the infrastructure for success, they cannot be held responsible for the inherent quality of the client's product or the ultimate outcome of the business "battle," which depends on the founders.
- **Handling Founder Comparisons**: Mahmud Bilow asked how to address potential objections from founders who have successfully raised capital without external assistance. Mohamed Anis suggested that comparing such founders to their clients is like comparing "apples and oranges," emphasizing that they provide the bridge if the client needs one, and there is no need to be different from those who do not require it.
- **Messaging Strategy and Content Refinement**: The team discussed the messaging strategy for outreach, including the use of one-liners and two-liners to communicate value effectively. Mohamed Anis and AHMED FARHAN agreed to utilize "Option Three" for messaging because it appeared more conversational and news-like, rather than a hard sales pitch. AHMED FARHAN noted that this new messaging should only be applied to new prospects, as restarting conversations with existing prospects could create confusion or doubt.
- **Client and Conversation Assessment**: Ahmed Farhan asserts that each client and conversation must be evaluated individually based on the specific context of their interactions rather than applying a standardized approach. Ahmed Farhan emphasizes that they should not treat these interactions as part of a repetitive or "finished" game, but rather approach each unique client situation as a distinct engagement.
- **Blog Feedback and Project Rollout**: Mohamed Anis discusses the reception of a recently published blog and proposes gathering input from team members or close contacts to identify potential areas of confusion. Mohamed Anis acknowledges that this feedback, which might highlight difficulties in understanding the content, is a natural part of the process. Ultimately, Mohamed Anis decides to proceed with a "full swing" rollout immediately, reasoning that feedback will be collected regardless of the timing and that the current work is sufficient to justify moving forward.
